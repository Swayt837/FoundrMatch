The matching engine

A number you can argue with.

Compatibility is computed, not guessed — six weighted dimensions, the same arithmetic every time, so two founders who see 74% are looking at the same thing. The AI doesn't produce the score. It explains it, in a sentence addressed to you and naming the person you're reading about.

Deal rooms

Matching is the easy part.

A match gives you a conversation. What you need next is somewhere to disagree productively — and a record of what you agreed.

Documents that stay yours

Upload the agreement rather than linking someone's Drive. Versions are kept, and replacing one clears the signatures — because signing v1 says nothing about v2.

Equity, settled in the open

A split that must total 100% across exactly the two of you, with vesting and a cliff. Neither side can revise it without withdrawing the other's acceptance.

Decisions on the record

Every decision needs sign-off from both founders. The point of a written log is that neither of you can later say you never agreed.

A plan that becomes work

A 90-day roadmap you can turn into real tasks in one tap, instead of a document you retype by hand.

Verification

You're about to discuss equity with a stranger.

Every badge is one less reason to hesitate — and we only issue the ones we can actually prove. A LinkedIn address someone typed is shown as a link, never as a checkmark, because the alternative devalues the badges that mean something.

Email

A code to the address on file. The floor for any account that will be handed a stranger's phone number.

GitHub

Proves control of the account. No repository access is requested — ownership needs public data and nothing more.

Website

A token placed on the domain claimed. Which is what "this is my company" means in practice.
